## 1) What this “awkward everyday comedy” bed actually is

There is not one single TikTok “awkward sound,” but there is a very recognizable family:

- **dry, mid-tempo comedy underscore**
- **a slightly sneaky / bureaucratic / unimpressed groove**
- **small musical gestures that leave space for dialogue**
- **a rhythm that keeps moving while the character is painfully slow**

For your dinosaur-developer sketch, the bed should feel like:

> “This man has been in meetings since 1998, hates every new initiative, and is taking seven seconds to reject a cookie.”

It should support the joke, not announce “THIS IS COMEDY MUSIC!”

### Tempo

Best range:

- **88–106 BPM** for slow dialogue and cynical deadpan
- Sweet spot: **94–100 BPM**
- Use a **straight 4/4 groove**, not a hyperactive meme tempo.

At 80 seconds, a consistent groove is useful because it gives the edit momentum even when the actor pauses. But it should not feel like a song people would dance to.

### Groove / feel

The strongest feel is usually one of these:

1. **Dry “sneaky office” hip-hop-lite groove**  
   Soft kick, rim click/snare, shakers, muted bass.  
   Not trap; more like a restrained breakbeat/boom-bap skeleton.

2. **Lightly funky corporate-comedy groove**  
   Muted electric guitar or Rhodes/Wurlitzer, bass, dry drums.  
   Important: “funky” here means restrained syncopation, not slap bass, wah guitar, disco, or party funk.

3. **Deadpan detective / procedural groove**  
   A simple bass ostinato, brushed or dry drum kit, occasional pizzicato/muted guitar figure.  
   Feels like something suspicious is happening — except the suspicious thing is that someone refuses a cookie.

The groove needs a little **forward motion**, but it should be emotionally neutral-to-cynical. Think: *the rhythm is mildly amused, while the character is not.*

### Rhythm section

A reliable rhythm bed:

- **Soft, short kick** — not huge/sub-heavy
- **Dry rim click or tight snare** on 2 and 4
- **Closed hi-hat/shaker** doing restrained 8ths or light syncopated 16ths
- **Warm electric bass or muted picked bass**
- Very occasional small percussion accent: muted clap, woodblock-like tick, low bongo/tom — but not Latin or cartoon percussion

Avoid a big modern trap kick, rapid hi-hat rolls, EDM claps, or giant cinematic drums. The drums should sound like they are sitting in an office cubicle, not in a trailer.

### Lead instruments

The safest lead palette for this exact comedy:

- **Rhodes or Wurlitzer electric piano**: dry, short chords or a tiny repeated motif
- **Muted electric guitar**: small plucked/choked notes, not rock guitar
- **Pizzicato strings**, but low and sparse — cello/viola more than bright violin
- **Vibraphone or muted marimba**, used very sparingly and in a lower/mid register
- **Bass clarinet, muted trumpet, or low woodwind** for occasional cynical phrases
- **Plucky synth only if it is soft, warm, and understated**, not shiny/cute

A good trick: have one instrument do a **small “questioning” 2–4 note motif**, then leave a gap. That gap is where the facial expression, silence, and cringe land.

### Harmony / mode

For “awkward but not cartoon”:

- Minor key, **Dorian**, or minor with occasional major-sixth color
- Simple 2–4 chord loop
- Use **minor 7ths, dominant 7ths, suspended chords, chromatic passing notes**
- Avoid overly happy I–V–vi–IV pop harmony
- Avoid exaggerated “silent movie” diminished-chord movement

Useful harmonic moods:

- **D minor / Dorian**: dry, skeptical, lightly sneaky
- **A minor with jazzy 7ths**: office sarcasm
- **E minor with chromatic bass movement**: “this meeting could have been an email”
- A repeating **i–iv–i** or **i–bVII–iv** loop often works well

The funny quality comes from tiny harmonic side-eyes: a slightly late chord, a chromatic bass note, a small unresolved phrase. Not from clown sounds.

### Production and mix

For vertical social comedy dialogue, the bed should be:

- **Dry to moderately dry**
- Warm, compact, and close
- Not wide and cinematic
- Not full of huge reverbs or dramatic transitions
- Mostly in the midrange, but carefully ducked around dialogue
- Light saturation/tape texture is good
- Low end controlled: enough bass to feel complete on a phone, not enough to cover speech

Useful mix characteristics:

- Drums: short, soft transients
- Bass: audible on phone speakers, not sub-only
- Lead motif: quiet and tucked behind speech
- No vocals, no spoken samples, no obvious comedic sound effects
- Leave frequent **micro-pauses** in the arrangement

For dialogue, generate music with a complete groove, then mix it roughly **-18 to -25 LUFS below the dialogue perceived level**, depending on delivery. The dinosaur’s slow deadpan lines need more space than a fast punchline sketch.

## Recognizable references / the family of tracks

The broader sound overlaps with royalty-free and YouTube-comedy staples such as:

- **Kevin MacLeod – “Sneaky Snitch”**  
  The classic “someone is doing something suspicious/stupid” comedy cue. Useful reference for sly rhythmic motion, but often too cartoony if copied literally.

- **Kevin MacLeod – “Scheming Weasel”**  
  More exaggerated, playful, and sneaky. The danger zone: this easily becomes children’s-cartoon music.

- **Kevin MacLeod – “Monkeys Spinning Monkeys”**  
  Very recognizable social-media comedy energy, but generally too cute/childish for your bitter veteran developer.

- **Kevin MacLeod – “Carefree” / light ukulele-stock territory**  
  Common YouTube background-comedy language, but likely wrong for this character because it says cheerful lifestyle vlog rather than corporate contempt.

- **“Curb Your Enthusiasm” / “Frolic” type awkwardness**  
  The famous awkward-moment sting. Great as a very short button/reference point, but too theatrical and distinctive to be the full 80-second bed.

- **Light corporate comedy / quirky crime-library tracks**  
  Often tagged: *quirky underscore, sneaky corporate, awkward office, dry comedy, light detective, playful tension, comedic suspense.*

What the good references have in common:

1. A clear, simple pulse.
2. Sparse instrumentation.
3. A small repeating motif.
4. Slightly offbeat or chromatic details.
5. Enough musical confidence to feel intentional.
6. Enough empty space for dialogue.
7. They treat the situation as mildly serious — which makes it funnier.

## 2) What usually goes wrong

### 1. It becomes “children’s comedy”

Your kazoo/tuba/ukulele problem is exactly this.

Common causes:

- Bright ukulele strumming
- Kazoo, slide whistle, clown brass
- Bouncy tuba bass
- High marimba/xylophone melodies
- Handclaps and cute percussion
- Major-key happy chord loops
- Too many comic sound effects

Those sounds tell the viewer: “Here comes a funny silly video.”  
Your sketch needs: “This is a normal office interaction, but this guy is unbearable.”

**Fix:** use adult instruments with comedic restraint: Rhodes, muted guitar, electric bass, dry drums, low pizzicato strings, bass clarinet, muted trumpet.

---

### 2. It becomes genre music instead of comedy underscore

Your trap, funk, afrobeat, synthpop, and jazz-hop attempts likely failed because each genre brings a strong social/emotional identity:

- **Trap:** swagger, menace, internet flex, modern coolness
- **Funk:** party, confidence, movement, groove-first energy
- **Afrobeat:** warmth, dance, celebration, uplift
- **Synthpop:** nostalgia, emotion, shiny pop structure
- **Jazz-hop:** chill/stylish/café/introspective mood

The sketch does not need a “vibe.” It needs a **functional comic pressure bed**.

**Fix:** borrow only tiny ingredients — for example, a boom-bap kick/snare pattern or muted funk guitar — but remove the genre identity. Keep it dry, sparse, and rhythmically plain.

---

### 3. It becomes too “sneaky cartoon villain”

Pizzicato strings and brass stabs are indeed in the right neighborhood, but they can quickly become:

- Tom-and-Jerry sneaking
- heist parody
- children’s mystery music
- exaggerated “uh-oh!” scoring

**Fix:** use pizzicato as texture, not as the main melody. Use low pizzicato cello/viola, short phrases, and only occasional brass punctuation. If brass is used, choose **soft muted trumpet or low trombone**, not big stabs.

---

### 4. It is too empty

Many “quirky” AI generations have only a plucked motif and no weight underneath. That can feel thin on a phone and leave the edit with no momentum.

**Fix:** always specify:

- kick + rim-click/snare
- warm bass
- shaker/hat texture
- chord instrument such as Rhodes/Wurlitzer/muted guitar
- one restrained comic motif

That gives you a full bed without clutter.

---

### 5. It is too busy

AI often hears “quirky comedy” and adds constant marimba runs, plucks, fills, and horn accents. That destroys deadpan timing.

**Fix:** explicitly ask for:

- sparse motifs
- long gaps
- no busy melody
- no fills every bar
- dialogue-first arrangement
- subtle changes every 8 or 16 bars only

The music should not react to every line. It should occasionally react to a look, a pause, or a final refusal.

---

### 6. It telegraphs every punchline

If there is a comedic sting after every sentence, the music becomes the joke. Deadpan comedy needs the actor to remain the joke.

**Fix:** reserve one slightly stronger musical turn for the final line or final reaction. Before that, let the bed stay mildly suspicious and patient.

## 3) Eight ready-to-use ElevenLabs Music prompt recipes

All recipes are designed as **instrumental dialogue beds**, not songs. Add this to every prompt if the model accepts it:

> Instrumental only, no vocals, no spoken words, no sung hooks. Dialogue-first mix. Dry, warm, understated office-comedy underscore. Avoid obvious punchline stings except near the ending.

## Best first choices for this specific “dinosaur developer” sketch

Start with:

1. **Recipe 1** — safest, most directly suited to cynical office deadpan.  
2. **Recipe 2** — best if the acting is very slow and uncomfortable.  
3. **Recipe 5** — best if you want it a little more modern and less “sneaky.”  
4. **Recipe 3** — best if the edit needs a recognizable awkward-comedy flavor.

For your description, I would avoid leading with bright pizzicato or comedy brass. Make the core bed **Rhodes + muted bass + dry rim-click beat**, then add only a little low pizzicato or muted guitar. That keeps it recognizably funny, but still adult, corporate, and cynical.
